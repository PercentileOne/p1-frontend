The Science
This isn't a new idea.
The research has known it for decades.

The learning science behind Explain is not a hypothesis. It is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive psychology. The method has a name — retrieval practice — and the evidence for it is overwhelming. We just built a product around it.

The Problem with Traditional Studying

"Rereading feels effective because the second encounter is easier than the first. The sentences move faster. Technical terms look familiar. The argument seems obvious. We interpret that smoothness as evidence that the information has entered memory. Often, the source is doing more of the work than we realise."

Familiarity is easily mistaken for knowledge. Highlighting, rereading, and note-taking all create the feeling of learning without producing durable memory. Research reviews have consistently found these techniques produce weaker long-term results than retrieval practice.

Dunlosky et al. (2013) · Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell (2013) · Soderstrom & Bjork (2015)
What Actually Works — And Why Explain Is Built On It

"A note proves that you encountered an idea. It does not prove that you can explain it independently."

Retrieval practice — the act of recalling and explaining information from memory, without the source — consistently produces stronger long-term retention than any passive study method. The more effortful the recall, the stronger the memory trace. Explain makes this the entire product: read, close the source, speak it out loud, get scored.

Karpicke & Blunt (2011) · Roediger & Karpicke (2006) · Cepeda et al. (2006)
The Feynman Technique
"What I cannot create, I do not understand." — Explain forces you to create an explanation. Under pressure. On the clock.
Spaced Repetition
Distributing practice across sessions produces dramatically better retention than massed "cramming" — the most robust finding in memory research.
The Generation Effect
Information you generate yourself is remembered far better than information you passively read. Speaking an answer generates it.
"If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough."
— Richard Feynman, Nobel Laureate in Physics

CV Interrogation

Your CV makes promises.
We make sure you can keep them.

Everyone exaggerates their CV. It's practically a sport. "Worked with Kubernetes" could mean anything from architecting a production cluster to once seeing the word on a colleague's screen. Explain reads the CV, finds every claim, and builds the exact questions a sharp interviewer — or a sharp recruiter — would ask. Before the room does.

Mode 1 · Candidate
Stress-test your own CV before the interview does.
Upload your CV. Explain generates 10–50 questions based entirely on what you've claimed — every role, every technology, every achievement. Answer them out loud. Find out where your gaps are before you're sitting in a glass-walled office trying to remember what you actually did at Barclays.
"You list Windows Azure at 14 months. Walk me through what you specifically built, and what you'd do differently today."
Mode 2 · Recruiter · Automated Due Diligence
Know if they're worth calling. Before you call.
Right now, the recruiter's day looks like this: find CV → gut check → call candidate → hope for the best → put them in front of the client → pray. With Explain, upload the CV and the job spec before picking up the phone. In 30 seconds: exactly where the candidate's claims don't match the role — and 15 sharp questions ready for the call.
Mode 3 · CV + Job Spec Combined
The exact interview mix. Before the exact interview.
Upload both a CV and a job spec together. Explain generates a blended interview — role questions based on what the employer needs, CV questions based on what the candidate claimed. This is exactly what real interviews look like.

AI Coaching

Not just scored. Coached.
Like a personal interview trainer in your pocket.

Scoring tells you what happened. Coaching tells you what to do about it. After every session, Explain's AI coach delivers personalised, actionable feedback — the kind that used to cost hundreds of pounds an hour from a human career coach. Competency-Based Interview Coaching
The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the gold standard for civil service, NHS, graduate schemes and corporate roles. Explain coaches candidates on structuring answers, choosing the right stories from their experience, and delivering them fluently under pressure — not just knowing the framework, but owning it.
💬
The Personal Questions Most People Fail
"Tell me about yourself." "What's your biggest weakness?" "Where do you see yourself in five years?" These questions trip up even the most technically brilliant candidates. Explain coaches the personal narrative — how to tell your story authentically, how to frame weaknesses as growth, how to sell ambition without arrogance.
🧠
Technical Explanation Coaching
For technical roles, being right isn't enough — you have to explain it clearly to a non-technical panel. Explain coaches candidates on layering technical depth: the simple answer first, the detail on request, the analogy that lands with a business stakeholder. The skill that separates senior engineers from junior ones.
📈
Personalised to Your Weak Points
After each session, the AI identifies your specific patterns — too many fillers, answers that trail off, missing the Action in your STAR answers, vocabulary that's too informal. Coaching targets exactly what that individual needs to fix, not generic advice that applies to everyone and helps no one.

The Panel Simulator

Each panellist has a personality.
And they're watching.

One evaluates everything you say. One goes three levels deep on every answer. One challenges you regardless of how well you're doing. Their reactions shift in real time — based on how you're performing.

🔵
The Evaluator
Scores every answer methodically. Impressed by structure and clarity. Neutral until you earn it. Becomes visibly warmer as your scores improve.
🟣
The Deep Diver
Follows up every answer with a harder question. Doesn't accept surface-level answers. "Can you go deeper on that?" is a good sign, not a bad one.
🔴
The Skeptic
Challenges everything. Not impressed easily. Every real interview has one. Explain puts them in every panel so you stop being surprised by them.
Filler Word Detection
Nobody forgets their score the first time they see "basically × 18."
Live detection of "um", "uh", "like", "basically", "you know". After every session, Explain shows your filler word count, tracks whether it's improving across sessions, and flags the moments they appeared in your transcript. Interviewers notice. Now you will too.
Surprise MCQ
Mid-interview. Countdown timer. No warning.
At a random point in your session, the panel pauses and drops a multiple-choice question from the job spec — timed. Real interviews test how you think under pressure, not just when you're comfortable. The Surprise MCQ makes sure you're ready for that too.
