Patent candidate list

Prepared for patent counsel · 2026-09-09
Following our call, here's the full list of everything we think may be worth evaluating — cast wide rather than pre-filtered, per your own steer that the combination is often where the real argument lives, not any single ingredient.
Context from our call: the sausage-machine example stuck with us — the meat guy and the skin guy both being individually "nothing new" doesn't stop the machine that combines them into something neither could argue against. Several items below are exactly that shape: individually unremarkable pieces (an AI call, a video avatar, a scoring rubric) combined into a specific sequence or mechanism that, as far as we know, nobody else has assembled this way. We've flagged which items are live in the product today versus designed-but-not-yet-built, in case that distinction matters for filing strategy.

01Interview delivery & format

The live interview session itself — how it's conducted, not how it's scored.
1.Dual concurrent real-time AI interviewer avatarsLive
Two independent, simultaneously-connected real-time video AI avatars — one HR-focused, one technical-role-focused — conduct one continuous interview session, handing off between each other live based on which question is being asked, rather than a single interviewer or a pre-recorded/sequential avatar.
Possible angle: the concurrent dual-session architecture itself, and the live handoff logic between two independently-running AI personas within one interview.
2.Mid-interview cinematic knowledge checkLive
A surprise multiple-choice knowledge test is woven directly into the flow of a live interview at a scripted moment (not a separate quiz module), introduced and reacted to by the AI interviewer in character, with correct answers adding bonus points that lift the candidate's overall interview score.
Possible angle: knowledge-testing embedded inside — and mathematically affecting the outcome of — a conversational interview simulation, rather than run as a separate assessment.
3.AI-decided dynamic follow-up probingLive
The same AI call that scores a candidate's answer also decides, in that same pass, whether the answer sounds too generic or unverifiable to be genuine — and if so, generates and puts to the candidate live (spoken, by the avatar) a specific follow-up question probing for a concrete detail. Aggressiveness scales with a selected difficulty tier.
Possible angle: an automated, real-time "detect a rehearsed or shallow answer and probe it live" mechanism, folded into the same scoring pass rather than a separate step.
4."Tell Me The Answer" reveal with structural anti-cheat lockLive
A candidate who doesn't know an answer can request a full AI-generated worked model answer instead of guessing or passing blind. The interface makes it structurally impossible to then read that answer back as their own attempt — the reveal is a blocking state whose only exit immediately advances to the next question — and usage is tracked and reported as a distinct metric, separate from genuine scored attempts.
Possible angle: the specific UI lock that prevents reveal-then-recite, combined with treating "asked to learn it" as a first-class, separately-tracked outcome type alongside pass/answer.
5.Candidate-directed topic focus ("With special focus on")Planned, not yet built
An optional field alongside the job title at intake where a candidate names a specific topic or technology, causing the AI's question generation to replace its default generic/CV-inferred questions with hard, targeted questions specifically on that named topic — layered on top of the same CV- and job-spec-driven generation already used for the rest of the interview.
Possible angle: candidate-directed narrowing of AI-generated interview content to a named topic, rather than only inferring focus areas automatically — the candidate steering the difficulty/subject matter of their own practice session in real time.

02Scoring, coaching & results

What happens to an answer after it's given.
6.Multi-dimensional real-time answer scoringLive
Each answer is scored live across independent dimensions (clarity, relevance, depth, confidence) plus a blended overall score, combined in the same pass with voice-delivery metrics — speaking pace in words-per-minute and filler-word detection — captured from the same spoken answer.
Possible angle: combining content-quality scoring and delivery-quality scoring from one spoken input into one unified real-time result.
7.In-session personalized AI coachingLive
Immediately after an answer is scored, a second AI pass generates specific coaching feedback that references the candidate's own answer, their CV, and the target job specification — delivered before the next question, not as a batch report at the end.
Possible angle: real-time, three-way-contextualised (answer + CV + job spec) coaching delivered mid-session rather than post-hoc.
8.Blended bonus-and-core scoring modelLive
Bonus points from the mid-interview knowledge check (item 2) mathematically lift the same headline score the core interview answers produce, rather than being reported as a disconnected side-note next to an unaffected percentage.
Possible angle: a single unified scoring formula spanning two structurally different assessment types (open-ended answers and multiple-choice) within one session.

03Learning & retention mechanic

The Learn Engine — using the interview format for general studying, not just job prep.
9.General-purpose "study via interview" methodLive
For any subject a user types in — not limited to job-interview preparation — the system AI-generates a full structured lesson (concepts, glossary, exam questions) and then interviews the user on that freshly-generated material, deliberately using the interview/testing format itself as the retention mechanic.
Possible angle: applying a job-interview-style conversational testing format as a general study technique for arbitrary subjects, with the lesson content itself freshly AI-generated per topic rather than drawn from a fixed curriculum.
10."Micro-subject" as the core discoverable unitLive
Hyper-specific study/practice topics (e.g. "Deploy a .NET app to Azure") are treated as the platform's primary searchable and shareable unit, rather than broad subject categories.
Possible angle: the granularity choice itself as the addressable/shareable content unit, distinct from how competitors structure course or topic taxonomies.

04Distribution & matching

How a completed interview reaches employers.
11.Record-once, broadcast-to-many distributionLive
A candidate completes one AI-conducted interview once, then distributes it to potentially many recruiters and employers simultaneously via a persistent shareable link and scannable QR code — replacing the need to repeat the same live interview separately for each employer.
Possible angle: this is close to the core "world's first Interview Broadcast Platform" positioning — the one-to-many distribution of a single AI-conducted interview session, as opposed to one-to-one video interviewing tools.
12.Two-sided live matching / alert engineLive
Recruiters and employers set standing criteria ("notify me when a candidate scores above X for role Y") that fire in real time as new interviews complete. Candidates simultaneously see a live feed of demand signals matching their own profile — a bidirectional system rather than one-directional job-board search.
Possible angle: the symmetry — both sides get live, standing, criteria-based push notifications from the same underlying event, rather than one side searching a static listing.
13.Reversible per-interview visibility with bulk toggleLive
A candidate can publish or unpublish any individual completed interview — or all of them at once — for recruiter discovery at any time after the fact, rather than a one-way "submit" action that can't be undone.
Possible angle: narrower than most items here, but the reversibility itself (versus every competing platform's one-way submission model) is a deliberate, arguably non-obvious design choice.

05Cost-efficient personalization at scale

Making expensive AI generation affordable per-candidate.
14.Shared personalized-greeting cache keyed by first nameLive
A synthesized, lip-synced "Hi [Name]" video greeting moment is generated once per first name and reused for every candidate who shares that name, rather than regenerating an expensive personalized video clip per individual candidate.
Possible angle: a specific caching architecture that makes personalized AI video economically viable at scale — the "key by first name, not by person" choice is the actual mechanism, not just "we cache things".

06Platform & access architecture

Weaker candidates than the above — more conventional engineering — included per your instruction to send everything.
15.Single neutral role-routing authentication gateLive
One shared login/registration entry point authenticates a user once and routes them into the correct one of several otherwise-independent applications (candidate, recruiter, employer, admin) based on their role, rather than each portal running its own separate authentication silo.
Possible angle: likely the weakest item on this list — single sign-on with role routing is a common pattern — but included since the specific "neutral gate" implementation across genuinely separate applications may still be worth a look.
16.Business-plan pitch as a formal interview typePlanned, not yet built
Treating a founder's business plan pitch as its own interview type, run through the same AI scoring/coaching apparatus (multi-dimensional scoring, live coaching, follow-up probing) built for job interviews.
Possible angle: extending the same underlying interview-and-score mechanism to a materially different use case (pitching, not job-seeking) — the transferability of the mechanism itself may be worth protecting ahead of building it.
17.Spoken, personalized "tell me about this role" career guidePlanned, not yet built
The AI interviewer gives a candidate a personalized spoken walkthrough of a specific career/role, drawing on that candidate's own profile and CV data, framed as a natural conversational answer rather than a static content page.
Possible angle: personalized spoken career content generated live from the candidate's own data, delivered in the same avatar/voice apparatus used for interviewing.
This list was compiled by reviewing the actual live product and its architecture — it's a starting inventory for evaluation, not a claim that any item here is in fact novel or non-obvious in the legal sense.
