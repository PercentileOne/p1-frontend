Ways into the platform, and the income behind each

Prepared by Francis Cobbinah, TheInterviewChair.com. 13 October 2026

The idea in a paragraph

Almost everyone, at some point, has to be interviewed, present, sit an exam or explain themselves out loud, and almost nobody gets to rehearse it for real. TheInterviewChair.com is the nearest thing to the real room: a live interviewer who speaks, listens and reacts, scored honestly, in 32 languages, private by default. Because the need is so universal, there is not one way into the platform but many. Each way brings a different kind of person and a different kind of income. This paper lists them all.

The ways in, one by one

1

Recruitment consultants

Who they are

Agency recruiters and in-house talent teams, in the UK and worldwide. Several million people do this job.

Why it fits them

A recruiter's income depends on one moment: the candidate performing well at the interview. Everything before it, the sourcing, the screening, the submission, is wasted if the candidate fumbles. Yet the support at that moment is usually "good luck on Thursday". Recruiters will see at once that it is best for them and for the candidate if the candidate goes in better prepared, having practiced for the exact role, based on the CV the client has already seen and thinks fits the job.

Our interviews are built from the job spec and the candidate's CV together, so the questions go straight to the gaps between what the candidate says on their CV and what the client wants. That gives the candidate the chance to close those gaps and practice thoroughly before the real interview, not during it.

The habit we are selling

Send a prep link every time you arrange an interview. That is all. It costs the recruiter about a minute and puts a rehearsal in the same message as the time and the address.

How recruiters find out about us
  • Direct sales: demonstrations to agency owners and recruitment managers, starting with people who already know and trust us.
  • Direct marketing: targeted email and LinkedIn outreach to recruiters and talent teams.
  • Advertising: LinkedIn and search adverts aimed at recruiters, and at candidates asking how to prepare for an interview.
  • Trade presence: recruitment industry bodies, trade press, conferences and webinars.
  • Word of mouth between consultants, and between agency owners.
  • Candidates asking their own recruiter, "can you send me one of these?"
  • The gift route in section 2, where a client's praise travels back to the recruiter.
  • Software partners: plugging into the systems recruiters already use.
What it could be worth

A seat per consultant each month, plus a small fee for each prep link sent. One saved placement fee covers an enormous number of preps. If every interview a recruiter arranges carries a prep link, the volume is the product.

2

Recruiters' clients (the hiring managers)

Who they are

The company people who receive candidates from a recruiter.

Why it fits them

A recruiter can give a client a free, branded set of interview questions as a gift. The client gets something useful, the recruiter looks generous and professional, and the client sees the platform in action without being sold to.

How they find us

Through their recruiter. It is the quietest, most trusted introduction there is.

What it could be worth

Every client who values it is a future employer account, and a reason for the recruiter to keep their seat.

3

Individual job seekers

Who they are

Anyone with an interview coming up: first jobbers, career changers, returners, people moving up.

Their story

Interview nerves are not a lack of ability, they are a lack of rehearsal. The platform lets you make your mistakes in private, get an honest score, hear exactly what to fix and go again, until the real interview feels like the fifth time and not the first.

How they find us
  • The free 3-question demo with no sign-up, reached from our homepage, LinkedIn, TikTok and other social posts.
  • The prep link a recruiter sends (section 1).
  • A gift from someone who cares about them (section 5).
  • A "Practice for this job" button on a job advert (section 4).
  • Search: people looking for "interview questions for [any job]" and "how to prepare for an interview".
  • Shared results and certificates: every candidate who shares a score, a link or a QR code on their CV or LinkedIn brings others in.
  • Printable question packs and the free CV analysis, each a small door into the platform.
  • The recruiter's own candidates asking, and employers pointing applicants to us.
What it could be worth

A monthly subscription, and the most important thing for the platform: a growing pool of practiced, scored candidates that employers and recruiters want to find.

4

Job adverts and careers pages

Who they are

Applicants reading a job advert on a job board, an agency site or an employer careers page.

Why it fits them

The most natural moment to practice is the moment you decide to apply. One click from the advert puts the applicant into a practice interview for that exact job, with the job spec already loaded.

How we get there

A "Practice for this job" button, or a simple per-job practice link a recruiter pastes into any advert. Recruiters can add it to every job they post. Job boards and employer careers pages can add it as a feature for their applicants.

What it could be worth

Per-job links for recruiters, and partnerships with job boards and employers, where every advert becomes a doorway.

5

Gifts

Who they are

A parent whose son has a big interview. A partner. A friend. A manager supporting a team member. A recruiter supporting a candidate.

Why it fits them

It is an easy, thoughtful and inexpensive present at a moment of real worry. The person receiving it gets a rehearsal without having to ask.

How they find us

Through our gift page, shared by anyone who has used it, and at the moments people look for help: graduation, redundancy, a new job search.

What it could be worth

Gift passes from a few pounds each, and every recipient is a new user who may subscribe.

6

Learners

Who they are

Anyone who wants to learn something, or pass something.

What they get

Type any topic and a complete course is built in front of you, with lectures, read-aloud, multiple-choice tests and spaced-repetition reminders by email. There are mock exams for a very wide range of professional certifications, and a Question Bank to save the answers worth revisiting.

How they find us

Search, the public Learn page, social posts, professional communities, and as a second step after an interview shows a weak area ("study this next").

What it could be worth

Subscriptions, and exam-practice partnerships (section 14). Learn also works as a staff benefit for employers, per seat.

7

Students and graduates

Who they are

Undergraduates, postgraduates, and people in their first months after leaving university.

Why it fits them

Universities teach the subject and rarely the interview. Graduate schemes, assessment centres, placement interviews and first jobs all need the same skill, and Talks lets them rehearse presentations and vivas as well.

How they find us

University careers services, student societies, graduate-recruitment events, social media, and the recruiters who place graduates.

What it could be worth

Individual subscriptions, and licences sold to the university itself, for every student from the first year to graduation and the years after.

8

Schools and colleges (ages 14 to 18)

Who they are

Pupils preparing for work experience, apprenticeship and sixth-form interviews, college and university admissions, and their first part-time job. Pupils in the GCSE years are at the point where careers education begins.

Why it fits them

A confidence skill is much easier to build at 15, with a patient AI that never judges, than at 25 after a string of rejections.

How they find us

Through schools, trusts, careers leaders and local authority careers services, with the school or parent as the buyer, and consent handled properly.

What it could be worth

A licence per school or trust. It could be our largest long-term group by sheer numbers.

9

Government and the public sector

Who they are

People who are unemployed, people out of work for a long time, people returning after long-term sickness or caring, young people not in education or work, veterans, care leavers, people leaving prison, refugees.

Why it fits them

What these groups so often lack is not ability, it is confidence, and the belief that an interview is something they can survive. A private practice room that never judges, in their own language, is exactly the right size of first step. It also suits the people who support them: work coaches, employment advisers, charities and training providers.

How they find us

Employment programmes and Jobcentre services, local authorities, health-to-work and occupational health services, the voluntary sector, and equivalent bodies in other countries.

What it could be worth

Programme and contract licences, where the payer is the public purse and the benefit is people getting back into work.

10

A global platform in 32 languages

Who they are

People who are most at a disadvantage when interviewing in their second language, or in a new country: migrants and refugees, international students, graduates in countries with large young populations and few careers services, and anyone who thinks and answers best in their own language.

Why it fits them

The interviewer speaks their language, so they can rehearse in the language they will actually be interviewed in. It opens a market that is a very large share of the world, not just the English-speaking job market.

How they find us

Local-language social posts, regional partners and universities, diaspora communities, and the same recruiter and job-board routes in each country.

What it could be worth

Every other stream in this list, repeated in each new market, with no new product to build.

11

Employers

Who they are

Companies and public bodies with their own hiring teams, large and small.

Why it fits them

Candidate Search lets them find candidates who have already chosen to be found, and who have been scored on how they actually interview, instead of reading a CV and guessing. Their recruiters can watch a full interview before spending time on a call. Their staff can also use Learn for development.

How they find us

Direct sales, referral by their recruiters (section 2), and by seeing candidates arrive with our certificate.

What it could be worth

A monthly platform fee, with Learn seats priced per staff member.

12

Closing the ghosting gap

Who they are

The millions of candidates who apply, interview and never hear back, and the employers who would like to reply but have no time.

Why it fits them

Being ghosted is one of the most common and most damaging parts of a job search. Because every interview on the platform is scored and written up, employers can give feedback without writing it, and candidates get something they can act on instead of silence.

How we get there

Feedback tools for employers, offered as part of their platform subscription, and a feedback-first approach that employers can promote as part of their brand.

What it could be worth

A strong reason for employers to pay, and a reason for candidates to choose employers who use it.

13

Training providers, bootcamps and coaches

Who they are

Colleges, coding bootcamps, apprenticeship providers, career coaches, outplacement and redundancy support firms.

Why it fits them

Their graduates and clients all need to interview well, and none of them can give every person unlimited, personal rehearsal.

How they find us

Direct approach, partner programmes, and referral through the recruiters who place their graduates.

What it could be worth

Resale or white-label licences, branded as the provider's own tool.

14

Professional bodies and exam providers

Who they are

Bodies that set professional exams and certifications.

Why it fits them

Candidates fail exams partly because they know the material but have never been asked it out loud. Our exam practice can sit beside any syllabus.

How they find us

Partnership proposals to the bodies themselves, and search from candidates preparing for a specific exam.

What it could be worth

Partnership and licence fees, and a steady flow of motivated paying users.

15

Talks and public speaking

Who they are

Founders pitching to investors, managers presenting to boards, people giving a best-man speech, students giving a viva, speakers, club members.

Why it fits them

Presenting is the same fear as interviewing. People rehearse out loud, get a score and a spoken debrief, and keep the talks they are proud of.

How they find us

From inside the platform, from speaking clubs and communities, and through social posts that show a before and an after.

What it could be worth

Subscriptions and group licences, and every public talk is a shop window.

Other ideas worth adding

  • Career changers and returners. People coming back after children, caring or illness, who have the most to gain from a safe first practice.
  • Internal promotions. Staff preparing for a step up, paid for by the employer.
  • Outplacement. Companies that make redundancies and want to give people something real, fast.
  • Armed forces resettlement. Leaving service personnel translating a military career into civilian interviews.
  • Neurodiverse candidates. A calm, repeatable, low-stakes way to learn the shape of an interview.
  • Apprenticeships and graduate schemes. Large employers preparing thousands of applicants at once.
  • Mobile. A phone-first app for the generation that does everything on a phone.
  • Sales and pitch practice. The same engine for a sales call, an investor pitch or a difficult conversation.

Everyone who has ever sat across a table from a stranger and been judged is a possible user. We reach them through the people who already stand next to them at that moment: their recruiter, their teacher, their coach, their employer, their friend.
