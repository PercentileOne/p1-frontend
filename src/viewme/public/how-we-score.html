Scoring & feedback

How we score every interview.

No black box. Every answer you give is scored across real dimensions, each one explained below with a genuine example — including one from an actual candidate's live session, mistakes and all.

The core four — every answer, every time

These four score every single answer in every interview, weighted into one overall percentage: Relevance 35%, Clarity 25%, Depth 25%, Confidence 15%.

Relevance35% weight
Does the answer actually address what was asked, and does it match what the role genuinely requires — not just talk in the right general direction?
Example
Asked how to keep a transactional endpoint consistent if a downstream step fails, an answer about general code readability would score low on relevance — however well written, it isn't answering the question that was actually asked.
Clarity25% weight
Is the answer well-structured and easy to follow, or does the candidate have to be untangled to find the actual point?
Example
"I'd wrap the write and the message in one transaction, and roll back if either fails" scores high on clarity — one clean sentence, the point is immediately obvious.
Depth25% weight
Are there specific examples, named tools, real numbers, or genuine outcomes — or is it generalities that could apply to almost any answer?
Example
"I'd make it robust" has no depth at all. "I'd use an outbox table so the message only ever publishes after the transaction commits" does — it names the actual mechanism.
Confidence15% weight
Does the language sound assured, or is it hedged with "maybe", "I think", "kind of" — even when the underlying idea is actually correct?
Example
"I think maybe we'd sort of use a transaction, possibly?" scores low on confidence even if a transaction genuinely is the right answer — the hedging undercuts it.
A real, unedited scored answer
"CivicForge Solutions asks you to expose an ASP.NET Core endpoint that creates a case and publishes a notification, but the database write and message publication must not leave the system inconsistent if either operation fails. How would you design the endpoint and surrounding components?"
"well I would definitely design them around a single transaction and roll back if there was an error"
Relevance
15%
Clarity
70%

5%
Confidence

Notice clarity and confidence both score well — it's a short, assured sentence — but relevance and depth are honest about the real gap: the transaction alone doesn't actually guarantee the message publishes, and the answer names no mechanism for that. That's the whole point of scoring these separately instead of one blended "good answer" number.

Leadership signal — two guaranteed questions, every interview

Every interview, regardless of role, includes two fixed questions asked by Amina (HR): "Tell us about a project or accomplishment you're most proud of," and "Where do you see yourself in five years?" Each is scored once, not on top of every answer.

Ownership & Executionfrom the "proudest accomplishment" question
Ownership — did the candidate personally drive or decide this, not just describe what "we" did? Execution — did they actually see it through to a real outcome, not just have a good idea that stalled?
Example
"Our team shipped a big project" shows neither. "I noticed the process was broken, proposed the fix, got buy-in, and shipped it three weeks later" shows both — the candidate is the subject of the sentence, and there's a real ending.
Proactivenessfrom the "five years" question
Genuine, specific forward planning and initiative — or a vague, clearly rehearsed non-answer?
Example
"Somewhere senior, I guess" scores low. "I want to be leading the platform team — I'm already picking up architecture reviews to get there" scores high, because there's an actual plan behind it.

Bonus round — a live knowledge check

Partway through most interviews, a surprise multiple-choice question fires — hard, role-specific, worth real points that lift the final score. Answered correctly, it genuinely raises the headline percentage; answered wrong, you get the full explanation on the spot and move straight back into the interview.
