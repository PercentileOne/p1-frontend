1. Background & Scope of This Revision

This brief supersedes the disclosure dated 8 August 2026, which presented nine claims spanning both the technical interview-generation engine and the surrounding two-sided marketplace (search, distribution, direct hiring contact, and a social "Like" mechanic).

On review — including direct comparison against HireVue (async video interviewing, employer-initiated, in market since the mid-2010s) and Voomer (text-prompted self-recorded practice with after-the-fact AI feedback) — several of the original nine claims were assessed as carrying material risk under UK/EPO patentability practice, specifically the exclusion of business methods and the "presentation of information" from patentable subject matter (Patents Act 1977 s.1(2); EPC Art. 52(2)) unless a claim demonstrates a genuine technical contribution beyond the automation of an otherwise-known business process on generic computing hardware.

The claims withdrawn from this disclosure — universal cross-channel distribution via shareable link/QR, multi-criteria candidate search, the direct-contact hiring workflow, the cross-platform identity graph, the trades-vertical application, and the "Like" social-engagement mechanic — remain commercially significant to the InterviewMe product but are assessed as better protected through trademark, trade secrecy, and first-mover execution than through patent claims that would likely fail on business-method or obviousness grounds. They are omitted here accordingly.

This disclosure presents only the four claims judged to carry genuine technical character: a specific technical mechanism, system architecture, or computational method — not merely a business outcome achieved via computer. A fifth candidate — an attribute-keyed caching architecture for personalized synthetic video ("Name Bank") — was considered and deliberately excluded from this revision as commercially minor and not worth the filing spend, notwithstanding its technical novelty.

2. The Core Technical System

The four claims in this disclosure describe three interlocking technical subsystems that together generate, deliver, and adapt an AI-conducted structured interview:

  • Generation (Claim 1) — an automated pipeline that captures, transcribes, and packages a candidate's interview session into a single verified data object, without manual curation at any stage.
  • Delivery (Claim 2) — a real-time orchestration engine that sequences multiple distinct synthetic interviewer personas across synchronized voice and video channels, with automatic session chapter-marking and dynamic mid-session content insertion.
  • Adaptivity (Claims 3 & 4) — two related mechanisms for making the interview session itself, and the candidate's development after it, responsive to real assessment data rather than fixed in advance.

Each claim is technically self-contained and independently patentable; none depends on the marketplace/discovery features omitted from this revision.

3. The Four Patent Claims

Each claim below is drafted around a specific computational mechanism, not a business outcome, per the scope note in Section 1.

Claim 1 [FOUNDATIONAL] — Automated Multi-Modal Pipeline for Verified Interview Object Generation

A computer-implemented method for automatically generating a structured, verified interview data object, comprising: capturing synchronized audio-video responses from a candidate during a structured interview session; processing the captured audio via automated speech-to-text transcription; automatically serializing the resulting video and transcript into a single structured data object; and automatically generating a persistent shareable identifier and machine-readable code for the object at the point of creation — the entire pipeline executing without manual data entry, transcription, or curation by a human operator at any stage. (Note: AI-based interview scoring is deliberately excluded from this claim — it is common in the art, see e.g. HireVue and comparable products, and asserting it would add nothing to the claim's novelty; see Section 4.1.)

Claim 2 [STRONGEST TECHNICAL CHARACTER] — Real-Time Multi-Persona Synthetic Interview Orchestration

A computer-implemented system for conducting a structured interview via two or more distinct synthetic interviewer personas, comprising: a phase-based state machine that sequences delivery of interview content across the personas; synchronized handoff between a pre-rendered video-avatar channel and a live text-to-speech audio channel, triggered by real-time session state rather than fixed timing; automatic timestamped chapter-marking of the resulting recording, keyed to persona identity and phase transition; and dynamic mid-session insertion of supplementary assessment content at computationally-determined points in the interview sequence, without interrupting or restarting the persona handoff sequence in progress.

Claim 3 — Real-Time Adaptive Follow-Up Question Injection Based on Live Response-Depth Scoring

A computer-implemented method for dynamically extending a structured interview session in response to answer quality, comprising: scoring a candidate's response in real time against a depth/specificity metric as it is received; comparing the computed score against a threshold conditioned on a pre-selected difficulty tier; upon the score falling below the threshold, automatically generating and inserting into the live interview sequence a supplementary follow-up question targeted at the specific deficiency identified in the response; and constraining insertion to a bounded maximum count per session to prevent unbounded session extension, the bound itself scaled to the pre-selected difficulty tier.

Claim 4 — Closed-Loop System for Interview-Derived Adaptive Curriculum Generation and Re-Assessment

A computer-implemented method for closing the loop between assessment and instruction, comprising: analyzing a completed structured interview's scored responses to automatically identify one or more subject-matter deficiencies; automatically generating a structured instructional module addressing an identified deficiency, without human authoring; tracking candidate engagement with the generated module; automatically triggering a follow-up assessment session on the same subject matter upon completion of the module; and computing and persisting a score delta between the original and follow-up assessment to a longitudinal candidate record, such that the generated instructional content is derived from, and its efficacy measured against, the candidate's own real assessment data rather than a generic curriculum.

Note on claim drafting: each claim above is phrased around a specific technical mechanism (a state-machine orchestration pattern, a scoring-triggered insertion rule, a closed data-feedback loop) rather than the business goal it serves. This is a deliberate response to the technical-character requirement under UK/EPO practice, and each claim should be reviewed and refined by the patent agent before filing — this brief establishes the technical substance, not final claim language.

4. Detailed Description of Key Claims

4.1 The Verified Interview Object Pipeline (Claim 1)

The patentable core of Claim 1 is the automated pipeline as a whole, not any individual step within it, and not the resulting data object in isolation — a data structure alone is not patentable subject matter. AI-based interview scoring is deliberately kept out of this claim altogether: it is standard practice across HireVue and comparable products, and including it would invite an objection over subject matter that adds nothing to the claim's novelty. The invention as claimed here is narrower and cleaner — the unattended, end-to-end chain from capture through transcription, serialization, and identifier/QR generation, executing with no human step anywhere in it. This distinguishes it from systems where curation or publication involves a human reviewer at any stage. Scoring remains part of the wider InterviewMe product but is intentionally outside the scope of what is claimed as inventive in this application.

4.2 Multi-Persona Orchestration (Claim 2)

This is assessed as the strongest claim in the disclosure. Neither HireVue nor Voomer — the two most directly comparable products identified to date — implement a synthesized-voice, video-avatar interviewer persona at all; both present interview prompts as static text. A system that orchestrates multiple distinct personas across a synchronized state machine, with real-time-triggered channel handoff and dynamic content insertion mid-session, has no identified prior art among current interview-practice or interview-assessment products.

4.3 Adaptive Follow-Up Injection (Claim 3)

The closest prior art category is computer-adaptive testing (CAT), which has used branching question logic based on live performance since the 1970s. This claim should be drafted narrowly around what is specifically novel relative to CAT: application to open-ended, AI-scored qualitative interview responses (rather than multiple-choice/scored-item CAT), and the bounded, difficulty-tier-scaled insertion cap as an anti-gaming mechanism. The patent agent should confirm this narrower framing survives a CAT prior-art search before filing.

4.4 The Closed Assessment-to-Instruction Loop (Claim 4)

"An AI generates a course on a topic" is not, alone, inventive. What is claimed here is the closed loop: automatic deficiency extraction from real scored-interview data → automatically generated instruction targeted at that specific deficiency → automatic re-assessment → automatic score-delta computation persisted against the same candidate record. It is the multi-stage, self-referential integration — not any single generation step — that constitutes the claimed invention.

6. Summary of Novelty

  • Unattended, end-to-end verified-object generation — capture through publication-ready serialization with no human step, distinguishing it from human-reviewed or human-curated systems including HireVue
  • Multi-persona synthetic interview orchestration — no identified prior art implements synchronized, state-machine-driven handoff between multiple distinct AI interviewer personas across video and live TTS channels
  • Depth-scored, bounded adaptive follow-up injection — application of adaptive branching to open-ended AI-scored qualitative responses, with a difficulty-scaled anti-gaming bound, distinct from conventional scored-item CAT
  • Self-referential assessment-instruction-reassessment loop — closed-loop integration where generated instructional content is derived from, and measured against, the candidate's own real assessment data
