## Option 1: LinkedIn post

When I started dialysis, I thought my life was over.

I hit rock bottom. More than once I sat in that chair and cried, wondering what the purpose of any of it was.

I'd been here before, in other ways. I was brought up in children's homes. I slept rough at seven. I've been homeless and jobless, with almost no qualifications, and plenty of people predicted I wouldn't see 27. At 29 I turned my life around, and for many years it went well, right up until I fell ill in 2022.

Then one afternoon, four hours into another session, something shifted. If I weren't here, I'd probably be switching off and letting the hours pass. But I could still use a laptop. I could still think. I could still plan. And one day I'll have my operation and won't need the chair at all.

So I started planning. That became TheInterviewChair.com.

It's a place to practice an interview out loud with live interviewers, in 32 languages, privately and with no camera, for anyone who has ever walked into a room unprepared, or lost their confidence after a long time away. Make your mistakes here, not in the real interview.

It's my legacy project, and very close to my heart. It's free to try, with no account: [link, with its own utm_campaign]

If you're sitting in your own chair today, whatever it is: you can still do something with the hours.
