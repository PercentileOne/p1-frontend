The problem

Almost everyone gets interviewed. Almost nobody gets to rehearse for real.

Interviews, presentations, exams and difficult conversations decide careers. The preparation on offer is a list of questions, or a friend, or nothing.

Questions are not an interview

Reading model answers is not the same as saying yours out loud to someone who reacts. Nerves fade only when the rehearsal feels real.

Briefing is not rehearsal

Recruiters brief candidates with the job spec and a call. Nobody can have twenty candidates answer out loud, and get scored, the night before.

The cost lands on the recruiter

The interview is the one moment that decides whether a fee is earned. The support at that moment is usually “good luck on Thursday”.

The product

The nearest thing to the real room.

1

Choose the job, company or subject

Any job or topic. Interview styles for 68 well-known employers. Built from the job spec and the candidate’s CV together, so the questions go to the gaps.

2

A live interview, spoken

Two interviewers (HR and technical) from a panel of five look at you, speak, listen and react. Ten or more questions, with follow-ups. Answer out loud or type.

3

Scored honestly

Five dimensions, a clear Pass, Keep-on-file or Fail verdict, and a spoken debrief. No black box: the weights are published.

4

A path to a pass

Weak areas lead straight to Learn, then “interview again”, with last time against this time. Interview, fail, learn, interview, pass.

Interview→Get scored→See what to fix→Go again

32 languages · phone and desktop · private by default: nothing is shared with recruiters unless the candidate chooses.

Every recruiter sends “good luck”. Let them send a rehearsal.

A prep link with every interview a recruiter arranges, in the message they are already sending.

1. The recruiter sends a link

With the job spec and CV loaded. About a minute of effort.

2. The candidate rehearses

Up to three spoken, scored practice interviews for that exact job. Free to the candidate.

3. They arrive ready

The result and certificate belong to the candidate, who can share them.

What we sell is realism: a live interviewer who looks at you, speaks, listens and reacts, not a list of questions.

What is not proven yet

The things a sensible person should ask about.

Not proven

  • Recruiter usage. No evidence yet of how many preps a recruiter will send.
  • Does it help win offers? We do not yet measure sent, opened, started, completed, second attempt and offer.
  • Per-prep billing is not built; recruiters pay through the seat fee today.
  • Paying customers. None yet.

The next 30 days

  • A pilot: 10 recruiters, up to 20 preps each.
  • Measure the attach rate: of the interviews arranged, what share carry a prep link.
  • Per-job practice links a recruiter can paste into any advert.
  • A short report to each recruiter: who practiced, how they scored.
